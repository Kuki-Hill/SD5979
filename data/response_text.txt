Student 1: How can I distinguish whether I have used ex-consequentia reasoning when
applying Naïve Bayes? (Figure 3)
When classifying something, there are two things to keep in mind:
- Feature
- Class (category)
A class is usually a larger set than 1 feature, the class overarches the feature
A feature is a smaller set than a class, is part of a class, a class is never part of a feature
A class is always equal or bigger than a given feature set
A feature set can be equal but never bigger than a class
Therefore, predicting a feature from a class is warranted (it must be in the set within the class)
> If human, then it has eyes
However, class attribution based on a feature is false unless only that class has that one feature
> If it has eyes, then it must be human (= false)
Why false: The class of animals also contains eyes, the class of robots also contains eyes, etc.

SS Total (Total Sum of Squares) tells the total variation in your data. It looks at how much the 
actual data points vary from the average value of y (the mean of all your y-values)
To calculate the SS Total, you take each data point, find the difference between that point and 
the mean of y, square these differences, and then add them all up. This gives you a number that 
represents the total variability in your data
In summary, SS Residuals tell you how much of the variability in your data is not explained by 
your regression model, while SS Total tells you how much variability there is in your data overall
The goal of regression is to minimize the SS Residuals, meaning you want your line to fit the data 
as closely as possible

121/176


PAGE 122
Even though the equations for a t-test and ANOVA look different, they are related in a specific 
way when you’re comparing just two groups
Here’s why t2 = F when comparing two groups:
A t-statistic indicates how much the means of the two groups differ relative to the variability in 
the data
An ANOVA with two groups provides an F-statistic, which is a ratio of the variance between
the groups to the variance within the groups
When you have only two groups, the result of the F-statistic turns out to be the same as
the square of the t-statistic. This is because both tests are essentially measuring the same 
thing: the ratio of the difference between group means to the variability within the groups
When comparing two groups, the t-test looks at the difference in means directly
ANOVA looks at the variance (the square of differences) between and within groups
So, for two groups, when you square the t, you get the F
Both assess the same relationship between group differences and variability

Central Credibility Intervals (CCIs) are used in Bayesian statistics to express the uncertainty 
around an estimate. They are similar to confidence intervals in frequentist statistics but have a 
slightly different interpretation
Think of it like this: Imagine you have a tank filled with air, and you’re trying to estimate how 
many molecules are oxygen. After doing some measurements, you might say, “I’m 95% sure that 
the number for oxygen is between 4,000 and 6,000 molecules per mm3.” This range (4,000 to 
6,000) is your credibility interval. It tells you where you believe the true number lies (t-test tells 
where!!!), based on the data and your prior beliefs

The Deviance Information Criterion (DIC) is a technique used to compare different 
statistical models (not effects!!). It helps you decide which model best fits your data while 
balancing complexity and goodness of fit
Think of it like this: Suppose you’re trying to design an educational game for school 
children. You want the game to help them learn (fit the data well) but also be simple 
enough to play (not too complex). DIC helps you find that balance (design trade-off) by 
giving you a score for each model. A lower DIC score suggests a better model

Unrestricted Regression Coefficients
In regression analysis (Lecture 08), coefficients represent the relationship between the 
independent variables (predictors x) and the dependent variable (outcome y). 
Unrestricted regression coefficients mean that there are no constraints placed on these 
coefficients during estimation (free fit)
Think of it like this: Imagine you’re trying to figure out how different factors (like hours 
studied and hours slept) affect your test score of the final exam (    ). Unrestricted 
regression allows each factor to have any possible influence on the score, without any 
pre-set limits. This means the analysis is free to find the best-fitting relationship based 
on the data
Restricted would be: First use hours studied (main factor) and then can hours of sleep 
(competing factor) explain more above and beyond what study hours explain
Put into relation, Central Credibility Intervals give you a range of plausible values for an 
estimate, the Deviance Information Criterion helps you choose the best model by 
balancing fit and complexity, and unrestricted regression coefficients allow for a flexible 
analysis of relationships in your data
